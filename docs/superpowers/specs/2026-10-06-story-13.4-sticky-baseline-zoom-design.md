# Story 13.4 Sticky Baseline Reader Zoom Design

**Date:** 2026-10-06
**Status:** Written design approved by the user on 2026-10-06; prototype tuning requires usability validation
**Story:** 13.4
**Baseline:** origin/main at 1eaebe7acba7f984a493488cf799ec40526f85cb

## Decision and scope

Reader zoom remains enlarged after the pinch ends. When pinching inward toward the normal fitted size, the content snaps to exactly 100 percent and resists further shrinking. Continuing the same pinch deliberately crosses that resistance and permits a smaller view. Releasing fingers does not automatically reset zoom.

This design replaces the product interaction and hard minimum in the original Story 13.4 plan. It does not select a production rendering mechanism, reopen a closed research timebox, or establish native acceptance. Preserve the existing zoom branch and reviewed research independently; do not cherry-pick its unfinished integration wholesale.

Dependencies are Story 13.1's chapter-scoped image pipeline, stable page geometry, and local progress persistence. No schema change, per-series zoom preference, new content source, offline retention, pagination, or panel detection is introduced.

## User flow

1. Open a chapter at its saved reading position and normal fitted size.
2. Pinch outward around a detail; the focal content stays under the gesture.
3. Release to keep the enlarged view. Drag pans the content; chapter progress remains frozen.
4. Pinch inward. Near the baseline, the reader settles at exactly 1x, preserving the reading anchor.
5. Either release at baseline to resume ordinary reading after geometry reconciliation, or continue closing the same pinch through a deliberate distance threshold to shrink below 1x.
6. If continuing below baseline, release to keep the chosen smaller view. Reset Zoom returns to the normal fitted size and the frozen reading anchor.

Persistent below-baseline scale is approved for the prototype, not a request for a whole-chapter overview. Smaller rendering must not trigger whole-chapter downloads, eager raster decoding, or eager materialization.

## Scale definition and initial tuning

100 percent is the current normal fitted reading size, including the selected fit mode, spacing, and usable viewport. It is not one source pixel per display pixel and is not the saved chapter progress percentage.

Prototype constants are 0.75x minimum, 3x maximum, and a downward snap entry at 1.03x. The 0.75x floor limits additional overview and loading demand. These values require usability tuning; do not add user-facing sliders or advertise an optimal threshold before validation.

Insistence is measured by continued inward finger movement, not pressure, elapsed holding time, pinch velocity, or event count. After the detent first engages, require a further cumulative inward magnification ratio of 0.88 before crossing below baseline. This corresponds to about 12 percent additional contraction from the engagement sample. Prefer an additive log-ratio accumulator so equivalent movement after a common engagement sample does not depend on callback count. Discarding the initial engagement sample's overshoot is an intentional safety exception: different sampled engagement points can change the required total movement. Native testing must evaluate that trade-off, not claim universal sampling invariance.

## Same pinch detent behavior

The policy has normal tracking, held-at-baseline, and crossed-below phases for one active pinch.

- While tracking downward from above baseline, reaching the snap band engages the detent and outputs exactly 1x. At most one engagement occurs until direction reversal rearms it.
- The sample that first engages the detent contributes no breakthrough distance. Discard its overshoot and begin distance accounting from that sample; a fast first sample must not jump through the stop.
- While held, further inward movement accumulates contraction. No new movement means no additional contraction. Reverse outward movement immediately abandons the hold, clears its accumulator, and resumes enlargement from 1x rather than an old raw scale.
- Before the 0.88 threshold, the output stays exactly 1x. At the mathematical threshold, enter the below-baseline phase from exactly 1x. Apply any contraction beyond that threshold in the current sample from the rebased scale; do not require another callback to shrink. This makes post-engagement breakthrough independent of event subdivision.
- The detent is bypassed for continued inward movement during that below-baseline phase. Clamp at 0.75x and rebase at bounds so direction reversal responds immediately without consuming invisible overshoot.
- When moving outward from below baseline, cross 1x without a hold or discarded outward movement; only downward movement has resistance. The geometry passes through normal alignment at baseline and continues to enlarge. Reversing inward at or above baseline rearms the downward detent.
- A new pinch started at 1x uses the same downward detent. A pinch started below 1x may shrink directly toward the floor. Holding or releasing below baseline does not fabricate an extra stop.
- Duplicate samples do nothing; nonfinite or nonpositive samples cannot mutate scale, anchors, or the contraction accumulator.
- Normal gesture completion commits the displayed valid scale. Cancellation restores the last committed presentation and anchor; neither path records reading progress during interaction.

Reset and chapter replacement clear detent state. Do not reset just because fingers are lifted at a valid enlarged or smaller scale.

## Gesture ownership and chrome

At idle, reconciled 1x, the chapter has exactly one vertical-scroll owner. During any pinch, detent hold, reset/reconciliation, or at a committed scale other than 1x, chapter scrolling and progress publication remain suspended. Merely observing scale == 1 is not permission to resume.

Off-baseline drag pans the bounded presentation rather than advancing chapter progress. For an axis with no content overflow, keep content aligned and do not invent a pan range. At below-baseline scale center horizontally; preserve the semantic reading anchor vertically and keep the configured Reader canvas visible in uncovered space.

The chapter's ordered content remains the same. Only the amount visible changes. Chrome stays fixed, retains View Original Page, and is not transformed. Consumed pinch, pan, reset, and double-tap gestures do not toggle chrome.

Double tap at idle 1x enlarges to 2x around the tap. Double tap at any off-baseline scale returns to 1x. Reset Zoom is available above and below baseline and while baseline reconciliation is pending. Zoom does not persist across leaving Reader or changing chapters.

## Position loading and progress safety

Preserve semantic page identity, normalized position within that page, and the inspected content point. On return to baseline, restore the frozen reading position, not the start of a page or chapter. Scale reset and zero translation alone cannot demonstrate this.

Geometry updates from decoded dimensions, settings, viewport changes, rotation, and insets must carry current session and revision identity. Queue them in one ordered reconciliation path; newer snapshots must not be overwritten by older queued updates. A late stored-position restore cannot replace a user-interacted presentation.

Allow normal progress only after the gesture has ended, actual layout is at baseline, the current reconciliation has completed, and the frozen anchor is measured against the current geometry. Gate ViewModel visibility, asynchronous readiness, repository writes, recent-reading writes, and completion effects—not only view callbacks. Inspect the actual progress call paths before integration.

Below-baseline visibility may include more images. Use one pipeline instance, preserve the maximum-three fetch/decode bound, and retain memory/materialization limits. Visibility changes during inspection cannot claim pages were read. Failures, retries, cancellation, and memory pressure remain page-local and do not reset the chapter.

## Architecture and implementation boundaries

A pure Reader interaction policy owns the detent phases, scale bounds, per-gesture contraction accounting, and valid sample handling. It has no SwiftUI, UIKit, loading, or persistence dependency.

A presentation adapter consumes policy output, applies focal geometry, and arbitrates gesture ownership. A separate progress/reconciliation policy controls publication. Reader views render state; existing services retain their repository and image-loading responsibilities.

No production scroll implementation is selected by this design. The earlier lazy-strip transform did not establish reliable native anchoring, and the native host remained specification-unapproved. Evaluate a single-owner candidate using owned image fixtures before production migration. Do not add a nested competing chapter scroll view or defeat lazy loading.

The first implementation slice is the pure detent policy and its tests. A package pass for that slice proves only policy behavior. Rendering, progress integration, native gesture feel, and production readiness are separate gates.

## iPad and accessibility

Use the same interaction contract for iPhone and any future native iPad reader. Treat the actual content viewport, not device class or screen diagonal, as the geometry input. Unit cases include tablet-sized and narrow-window viewports; these do not prove native iPad support.

Current MVP remains iPhone-first and the app target is iPhone-only. This revision does not change device-family settings, introduce tablet navigation or spreads, or claim iPad validation. Native iPad support requires a separate scoped design and device matrix.

Provide non-pinch accessibility actions to enlarge, shrink, and return to normal. An explicit shrink action may select the prototype 0.75x view without requiring the pinch detent; enlargement may select 2x. Actions and VoiceOver focus must remain usable off-baseline even while progress is frozen. Actual VoiceOver verification remains required for Reader acceptance.

Reduce Motion preserves the same states and positions without spring/bounce/reset animation. Haptics are not needed to cross the detent and are not acceptance evidence. iOS 17 runtime coverage remains waived by the user; deployment/API availability stays unchanged.

## Acceptance and evidence

Required pure tests cover ordinary scale tracking; snap-band entry; exact 1x hold; deliberate same-pinch crossing; first-sample overshoot; stationary and duplicate samples; equivalent dense/sparse post-engagement movement from a common engagement point; preservation of excess contraction in a threshold-crossing sample; reversal/rearming; bounds rebasing; invalid values; reset; completion; and cancellation from above and below baseline. Separately test and disclose the initial overshoot exception rather than requiring incompatible global sampling invariance.

Native gates exercise slow and fast pinches, baseline release, deliberate breakthrough, pan, double tap, reset, chrome coexistence, short chapters, long chapters, first/last-page bounds, settings and inset changes, delayed real dimensions, delayed initial restore, retries, memory pressure, session replacement, and stale callbacks. Measure real content positions rather than trusting cached offsets or a zoom percentage label.

Retain the existing fidelity gate: the actual converted semantic anchor must be within 2 points on each axis of its required position; settled/reset measurements must remain within that tolerance for at least one continuous second. This interval is an evidence check, not a production delay or timeout-based acknowledgement. For an unchanged decoded target, retain the existing four-frame-value comparison. If decoded dimensions change, compare the same normalized page point in the true new geometry rather than requiring an obsolete image height. During an active geometry transaction the same 2-point continuity limit applies to the current/focal point. Verify decoded/hittable and reachability preconditions first; invalid or unavailable scenarios are inconclusive, not passing evidence. Do not widen tolerance, use cached-point assertions, or relabel a failed precondition as an isolated anchor failure.

Before any production integration, the two dedicated current-runtime iPhone simulators must pass measured anchor fidelity, loading/residency/concurrency checks, strict repository-spy progress isolation, actual VoiceOver, and system Reduce Motion. No blanket evidence waiver follows from changing the gesture.
